Teaching

Learning that is supported, hands-on, and shared.

I don’t want students to memorize marketing — I want them to use it. My courses move real business problems from data to decision, so students leave with the confidence to face unfamiliar questions, the habit of asking for evidence, and the experience of working it out together.

Three principles
01

Supportive

Students learn best when questions are welcome and difficulty is treated as part of learning — not evidence they can’t. I keep expectations high and doors open: in class, after class, by email, and in office hours, with feedback that motivates rather than discourages.

02

Experiential

Marketing is an applied discipline. My Marketing Research course is built around a semester-long project on a real business problem — from problem definition through data collection and analysis to final recommendations — so every technique has a purpose students can see.

03

Collaborative

Marketing problems are rarely solved alone. Students work in teams throughout the research process — negotiating ideas, dividing responsibilities, learning from each other — while individualized, actionable feedback keeps each student’s own growth visible.
