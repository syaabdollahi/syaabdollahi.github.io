About

Convenience always asks something of us.

Modern consumption rarely ends at the checkout. We subscribe and renew, share access, and live alongside technologies that learn from us. I study what that asks of people.

My work examines how consumers experience digital and technology-enabled market offerings — the quiet burden of managing many recurring commitments, the sense of fairness and ownership that access-based models can strain, and the beliefs, emotions, and trust that shape how we welcome intelligent machines into everyday life. I am problem-focused and methodologically flexible: interviews and netnography, experiments, text analysis, and biometric tools like eye-tracking.

My goal is research that helps firms design markets that feel better to live in — and that takes consumer well-being as seriously as consumer spending.

What I study

Recurring consumption

Subscription fatigue, coping, fairness, and psychological ownership — what it costs consumers to live inside portfolios of recurring commitments.

What connects it

Living with technology

From locked car features to home robots, my projects share one question: how do people balance convenience and control in technology-mediated markets?

Why it matters

Markets worth living in

Findings that advance consumer theory while giving firms and policy a consumer-centered view of responsible subscription and technology design.
